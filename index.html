How the forecast works

Three steps, from raw reports to a risk call

  • 1
    Time-series forecast. A damped trend model (fit in log-space) plus a Random Forest on rainfall/temperature, ensembled together, projects expected case counts for the next 4 weeks.
  • 2
    Two risk signals, kept separate. A severity badge shows how large the case count is in absolute terms; a trend arrow shows whether it's rising or falling relative to that district's own recent pattern. A district can be severely affected but stable, or small but rising fast — those aren't the same thing.
  • 3
    You pick a district. See its 8-week trend, forecast, and confidence range — like a weather forecast, but for dengue.

How this works

Forecasts combine a damped trend model fit in log-space with a Random Forest regressor trained on rainfall and temperature, using weekly dengue case counts from the Epidemiology Unit / NaDSys, Ministry of Health Sri Lanka, alongside weather from Open-Meteo. Risk labels compare each district's forecast to its own recent baseline, not a fixed cutoff, since "high" means something different in Colombo than in Mullaitivu.
